How the model works

  • Starting point. Both structures start from the 9/22/2026 roster of 2,494 paying members, grown by the roster growth rate.
  • Member response. A setting of 4% means each 10% increase loses 4% of members, combining fewer sign-ups and fewer renewals. In tier mode it applies to each tier's price. On a sliding scale it applies to the rise in the average amount paid compared with today's $37.54. The effect is a straight line, which gets less reliable for large changes.
  • Tier mode. Part of the members lost from Tool Box and Wheelbarrow move down one tier. Upgrading members come out of Tool Belt and pay the Tool Box price. SIF members pay double; their full payment counts as revenue, as in the deck, and the extra half goes into the scholarship fund.
  • Sliding scale. Each member pays the floor, the suggested amount, or the sustaining amount. Peers vary widely: Chicago's pay-what-you-can averages about $35 and Station North's sliding scale about $75, so the suggested amount and how it's presented matter a lot.
  • Project pass. Members who switch stop paying their annual amount and buy passes instead. New pass buyers add revenue without replacing anyone.
  • Scholarships count as members but add no revenue. Their cost is drawn from the SIF at the Tool Belt price, or at the floor on a sliding scale.
  • Costs are fixed. More or fewer members don't change program costs here. In practice repairs and waitlist pressure rise with use.
